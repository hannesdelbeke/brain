## the two gaps this closes

[[2026-09-01 wikipedia hop-ceiling test - setup and reproduction steps]] added closed-book screening to rule out a model already knowing the target fact — a real fix, but it only tests *recall*. It doesn't rule out that prior exposure to a *topic* (even without knowing the specific number) makes a passage easier to recognize once shown, which would still quietly help a position test look cleaner than it should.

Separately: the whole test — content, questions, protocol — was designed by Claude, and the agents doing the reading are also Claude (Haiku, Sonnet). Content one model writes can be systematically easier for a related model to parse: shared conventions about where a fact sits in a sentence, what "the important clause" looks like. That's the same shape of bias as self-preference in LLM-as-judge research, just between writer and reader instead of judge and candidate.

Full design, exact content, and protocol: [[2026-09-01 hop-ceiling test vaults repo|hop-ceiling-test-vaults]] (`C:\Users\H\Documents\GitHub\hop-ceiling-test-vaults`, its own git repo, not part of this vault).

## the fix

- **entirely invented content** — two topics that did not exist before this test was written, so no closed-book screening is even needed to rule out memorization (it's guaranteed by construction). Still ran a screen for the record; both models said "I don't know," as expected.
- **cross-model authorship for the two target facts** — a fictional handheld tool ("quantum ruler," 0.0001 inch resolution) and a fictional atoll ("Karrivale Atoll," highest point 12 m above sea level), both generated by `openai/gpt-oss-20b` via the Groq API from a short creative brief, not written by Claude. The six distractor notes per vault stayed Claude-authored — lower stakes, since a distractor only needs to be a plausible unrelated topic, not the thing accuracy is measured against.
- **the same code/serial-number trap avoided again** — both target facts are described in plain prose (a resolution figure, an elevation figure), not a unique alphanumeric key, matching the original diagnosis from [[2026-09-01 why the u-curve disappeared in candidate 2's multi-document test]].
- **two vaults, one variable isolated** — `vault-short` (1–3 short sentences per note, the terse register the very first hop-ceiling pilot used, where trouble first showed up) and `vault-long` (~150–250 words per note, Wikipedia-article length and register), entirely different fictional topics between the two so nothing in one could leak into the other.

## result

positions 1, 4, and 7 of a 7-hop chain, one trial each, Claude Haiku, both vaults: **6/6 correct, zero trouble anywhere.** full route table in the repo's `RESULTS.md`.

this matters specifically because `vault-short` was built to test the one variable the wikipedia round couldn't isolate: is *terseness itself* what caused the original vault-note pilot's trouble, once memorization and writer-reader bias are both controlled for? the answer, at least at this sample size, is no — terse invented content behaved exactly like long invented content, and both behaved exactly like screened wikipedia content: cleanly.

## what this leaves standing

across every round of this research line now:

- **real personal vault notes** — real trouble (roughly 1-in-6 at a middle hop across 12 trials; one full unrecovered miss at hop 1 in a 13-hop chain)
- **real wikipedia paragraphs, screened** — zero trouble, all 7 positions, 2 models
- **invented short notes, cross-model target** — zero trouble, 3 positions
- **invented long notes, cross-model target** — zero trouble, 3 positions

three separate controlled conditions all came back clean; only the original, real, personal-vault condition ever found anything. the most honest reading left standing is that the original trouble was likely specific to something about *that* material — near-duplicate topics, ambiguous personal shorthand, or a small-sample noise event — rather than a general property of short notes, long notes, or turn-based amnesia by itself.

**the one gap this note doesn't close:** sample size. 3 trials per vault this round against the original's 12 means a true ~1-in-6 rate could easily land on 0/3 by chance. the next honest step, if anyone continues this, is matching sample sizes — repeating the synthetic short vault at the same n as the original before treating "terseness doesn't do it" as settled rather than "not yet found."
