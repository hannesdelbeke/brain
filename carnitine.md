Carnitine is a substance that helps the body turn fat into energy. 

Your body makes it in the [[liver]] and [[kidneys]] and stores it in the [[skeletal muscles]], [[heart]], [[brain]], and [[sperm]]. Usually, your body can make all the carnitine it needs, even vegetarians.

Carnitine is a quaternary ammonium compound involved in [[metabolism]]

L-carnitine supplementation has a modest effect on [[weight loss]]: a meta-analysis of 37 randomized trials in 2,292 people found about 1.21 kg more weight loss than placebo, but the fat mass and BMI effects didn't hold up once the analysis was limited to the higher-quality trials ([study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32359762/)). the brain function and disease prevention claims often made for carnitine mostly come from research on acetyl-L-carnitine, a related but distinct compound studied for specific conditions like depression and neuropathy, not plain L-carnitine taken for general health, so that claim doesn't carry over.
Supplements may also benefit those with lower levels, such as older adults, vegans, and vegetarians. ([[vegetarian macros]])


> [!NOTE]- L vs D carnitine
> Carnitine exists as one of two [stereoisomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoisomer "Stereoisomer"): the two [enantiomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer "Enantiomer") d-carnitine (_S_-(+)-) and l-carnitine (_R_-(−)-). Both are biologically active, but only l-carnitine naturally occurs in animals, and d-carnitine is toxic as it inhibits carnitine acetyltransferase, blocking the l-form's normal function ([study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26457589/))
