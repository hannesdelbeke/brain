Autism is a neurotype defined by distinct cognitive architecture—how information is processed, filtered, and prioritized—rather than a list of social deficits. Modern research frames it through several core mechanisms:

### Monotropism
The brain allocates deep processing power to one interest channel at a time, filtering out everything else. This isn't just [[hyperfocus]]—it's the underlying architecture that makes hyperfocus possible. When the channel is active, peripheral life (tidiness, physical needs, secondary conversations) genuinely drops from awareness. See nervous system regulation dynamics for how monotropic flow vs fragmented context switching affects nervous system regulation.

### Bottom-Up Perception
Neurotypical brains run heavy top-down prediction: "I expect a face, so I see a face." Autistic perception processes raw sensory and logical data first, assembling the picture from granular detail upward. This produces exceptional pattern detection and edge-case spotting (auditory and sensory sensitivity reflects this too), but the lack of automatic compression means the brain handles far more raw input, increasing both cognitive load and sensory vulnerability.

### Cognitive Inertia
Flows directly from monotropism. Starting, stopping, or redirecting an active cognitive tunnel costs significant executive energy. Interruptions aren't annoying in a preference sense—the entire mental model has to be demolished and rebuilt. Context switching between fragmented admin tasks vs deep single-track work produces measurably different stress responses, as documented in nervous system regulation dynamics. See also [[Activation Energy Minimisation]] and [[decision fatigue]] for related friction mechanics.

### Atypical Interoception
Weakened or delayed perception of internal body signals: hunger, thirst, bladder pressure, temperature, fatigue. During deep focus these cues get filtered out and only register at acute thresholds. Evidence in deep hyperfocus states (hunger cramps from eating too fast, sore bladder from dehydration, procrastinating showers for 2+ days during a hyperfocus sprint). Overlaps heavily with [[ADHD]] executive neglect of body maintenance.

### The Double Empathy Problem
Damian Milton's framework: social friction isn't a one-way autistic deficit but a bidirectional mismatch. Autistic-to-autistic communication is typically direct, literal, and high-bandwidth. Autistic-to-neurotypical mismatch happens because NTs rely on unstated subtext, tone calibration, and phatic signaling, while autistic communication prioritizes accuracy and informational density. See [[3 conversation categories]] for the "helped, heard, or hugged" framework and conversational rule modeling for manualized conversational rules.

### Hyper-Systemizing
Simon Baron-Cohen's Systematizing Theory: the core autistic drive to analyze, categorize, and optimize rule-based systems. Manifests as folder taxonomies, pipeline tools, dashboard architectures, card game min-maxing, and manualized social strategies (rule-based domain architectures). The interest is the architecture and rule structure itself.

### Masking & Camouflaging
Running social interaction through conscious executive function: calculating eye contact duration, modulating vocal tone, scripting conversational beats, suppressing stims. High metabolic overhead produces post-social fatigue and long-term burnout risk. masking studies documents the double life of masking—the organised version others see vs the private struggle. [[leveraging the Big Five for contentment and growth]] notes the conscious mode-switch from "engineering audit" to "presence" filter.

### Parallel Play & Info-Dumping as Connection
Autistic intimacy often runs through shared deep-focus activities (drawing side by side, coding together, board games) or enthusiastic knowledge sharing, rather than open-ended phatic chatter. shared activities captures this directly: connection through quiet shared activity, with friction when it's disrupted. shared technical passions shows how bonding happens through shared technical passions while generic small talk feels hollow.
