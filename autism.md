Autism is a neurotype defined by distinct cognitive architecture—how information is processed, filtered, and prioritized—rather than a list of social deficits. Modern research frames it through several core mechanisms:

### Monotropism
The brain allocates deep processing power to one interest channel at a time, filtering out everything else. This isn't just [[hyperfocus]]—it's the underlying architecture that makes hyperfocus possible. When the channel is active, peripheral life (tidiness, physical needs, secondary conversations) genuinely drops from awareness. Monotropic flow vs fragmented context switching directly affects nervous system regulation.

### Bottom-Up Perception
Neurotypical brains run heavy top-down prediction: "I expect a face, so I see a face." Autistic perception processes raw sensory and logical data first, assembling the picture from granular detail upward. This produces exceptional pattern detection and edge-case spotting (sensory and auditory sensitivity reflects this too), but the lack of automatic compression means the brain handles far more raw input, increasing both cognitive load and sensory vulnerability.

### Cognitive Inertia
Flows directly from monotropism. Starting, stopping, or redirecting an active cognitive tunnel costs significant executive energy. Interruptions aren't annoying in a preference sense—the entire mental model has to be demolished and rebuilt. Context switching between fragmented admin tasks vs deep single-track work produces measurably different stress responses. See also [[Activation Energy Minimisation]] and [[decision fatigue]] for related friction mechanics.

### Atypical Interoception
Weakened or delayed perception of internal body signals: hunger, thirst, bladder pressure, temperature, fatigue. During deep focus these cues get filtered out and only register at acute thresholds. Overlaps heavily with [[ADHD]] executive neglect of body maintenance during deep hyperfocus.

### The Double Empathy Problem
Damian Milton's framework: social friction isn't a one-way autistic deficit but a bidirectional mismatch. Autistic-to-autistic communication is typically direct, literal, and high-bandwidth. Autistic-to-neurotypical mismatch happens because NTs rely on unstated subtext, tone calibration, and phatic signaling, while autistic communication prioritizes accuracy and informational density. See [[3 conversation categories]] for the "helped, heard, or hugged" framework and explicit conversational rule modeling.

### Hyper-Systemizing
Simon Baron-Cohen's Systematizing Theory: the core autistic drive to analyze, categorize, and optimize rule-based systems. Manifests as folder taxonomies, pipeline tools, dashboard architectures, and min-maxing rule-based domains. The interest is the architecture and rule structure itself.

### Masking & Camouflaging
Running social interaction through conscious executive function: calculating eye contact duration, modulating vocal tone, scripting conversational beats, suppressing stims. High metabolic overhead produces post-social fatigue and long-term burnout risk. Masking creates a double life—the organized external presentation vs the private metabolic drain.

### Parallel Play & Info-Dumping as Connection
Autistic intimacy often runs through shared deep-focus activities (drawing side by side, coding together, board games) or enthusiastic knowledge sharing, rather than open-ended phatic chatter. Connection happens through shared technical passions and side-by-side creation while generic small talk feels hollow.
