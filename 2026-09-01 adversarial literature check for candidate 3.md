> candidate 3 argues three things: multiplying or rank-fusing a recency signal into search ranking reliably backfires, a small additive version reliably works, and the same failure mode explains why "who touched this code most recently" is a weak expertise signal. this note went looking for research that argues the other way on each. none of the three claims falls over. the multiplicative and rrf claims both survive because every counter-example found either doesn't run the specific head-to-head test candidate 3 runs, or defends rrf for a different combination problem (fusing two relevance rankers) than the one candidate 3 tested (fusing relevance with a non-relevance signal). the git-authorship claim survives too, but the search found a real problem worth fixing first: the paper's own cited source for "recency of modification predicts expertise" appears to say the opposite — recency of modification has the *strongest negative* correlation with measured code knowledge in that literature, not a second positive predictor alongside first authorship.
> **needs from you:** approve the three limitations additions made directly to [[2026-09-01 candidate 3 paper draft outline]] — recommend yes on all three: drop or replace the re3 citation (it doesn't use rrf and was withdrawn), add zorro as a third multiplicative-wins-in-production counterexample with the same "no head-to-head ablation" caveat as elastic and temporal-rag, and verify the codecv/montandon correlation direction against the primary source before submission since the current wording may have it backwards.

> adversarial research task: find real published research or credible production systems that argue against or complicate candidate 3's three claims (multiplicative/rrf recency combination fails, additive combination works, this generalizes to code-authorship expertise-finding), beyond the elastic and temporal-rag counterexamples the paper already cites. for each finding, decide whether it actually contradicts the paper's specific wikilink-ground-truth, rival-density mechanism, or operates under different enough conditions not to be comparable.

## claim 1: multiplicative/rrf recency combination reliably fails

false alarm, with one real addition to the paper's own counterexample list. [zorro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10910) (verified 2026-09-01), a 2026 news-recommendation paper, combines an exponential recency-decay "intrinsic relevance" term with a cosine-similarity "relational relevance" term as a hadamard product — multiplicative, not additive — and reports beating strong neural baselines in offline ranking while running over 600x faster. that's a third real system, alongside [[2026-08-31 recency-proximity reranking prior tested against real wikilinks#prior art on the combination question|elastic and emmimal/temporal-rag]], where multiplicative wins in production.

it doesn't threaten candidate 3's claim the way it first looks like it might. zorro's own sensitivity analysis sweeps decay rates (λc, λh) and which similarity components to include, never additive-vs-multiplicative combination directly — the same gap already true of elastic's blog post and temporal-rag's design choice. none of the three production counterexamples run the head-to-head ablation candidate 3 runs against real wikilink ground truth. they show multiplicative can win somewhere, not that it beats additive on the same task and data.

a second, more interesting complication: [tofallis, "add or multiply?"](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3762021) (informs transactions on education, 2014) (verified 2026-09-01) argues multiplicative aggregation is generally superior for combining multiple decision criteria — it avoids a normalization dependency that changes rankings depending on arbitrary scaling choices, and it gives a real veto property: a catastrophic score on any one criterion zeroes the total, the way one bad measurement can't be compensated by others. this is a genuine theoretical voice for multiplicative, from a field unrelated to search, which is exactly the kind of source candidate 3's own robust-control and bayesian-odds arguments are trying to out-argue. it doesn't transfer cleanly, though: tofallis's case is built for criteria meant to jointly gate a decision, where a candidate should fail if any one factor is bad (a diamond needs every quality dimension to be good, not just one). candidate 3's mechanism is the opposite shape — a diffuse, non-veto signal (temporal proximity) should never be allowed to zero out or override a specific, already-strong content match. worth citing as the strongest available theoretical counterweight, and worth stating plainly why the veto property argues against multiplicative here rather than for it.

## claim 2: rrf is a reliable, defensible default for this combination

false alarm, but the paper's own citation supporting the rrf test needs fixing regardless. rrf is not a fringe choice — [cormack et al.'s original 2009 sigir paper](https://cormack.uwaterloo.ca/cormacksigir09-rrf.pdf) validates it empirically across trec submissions and the letor 3 benchmark, and production search infrastructure (elasticsearch, opensearch, weaviate, qdrant, azure ai search) ships it as a default or first-class hybrid-search fusion mode. the voting-theory argument for why it's robust is real: rank position is stable across systems with incomparable score scales, and the constant k caps how much any one system's top pick can dominate.

every one of those validations, though, tests rrf fusing multiple relevance rankers — bm25 against a dense vector index, or several learned-to-rank models — each already a reasonable proxy for the same relevance judgment. none of the sources found test or even discuss rrf fusing a relevance ranker against an orthogonal, diffuse signal like temporal proximity, which is candidate 3's actual claim and the rank-flip proof's actual precondition: a temporally-clustered rival with a mediocre content rank getting pushed ahead by a strong recency rank, not two decent-but-disagreeing relevance judgments converging. a 2026 e-commerce learning-to-rank paper on multi-channel retrieval does criticize standard rrf's fixed global weights as suboptimal for balancing freshness against relevance and popularity in production, favoring a learned alternative — real complicating evidence, but it argues against naive rrf in general, not for it, and doesn't test the specific mechanism either. rrf's real-world track record is evidence for a claim candidate 3 isn't making, not evidence against the one it is.

one thing does need fixing regardless of the mechanism question: candidate 3's method section justifies testing rrf because "a 2025 paper (re3, arxiv 2509.01306) uses it specifically to cap unbounded displacement." checking the actual paper: [re3's mechanism is a learned, query-aware gating function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01306), not rrf — the term "reciprocal rank fusion" doesn't appear in its text at all — and the paper was withdrawn in january 2026, with the authors stating the current version "no longer reflects the direction" of their revised work. this citation should be removed or replaced. it doesn't affect the rrf rejection itself, which stands on candidate 3's own k-sweep (k=1 to 100,000, never positive) rather than on re3's authority, but citing a withdrawn paper for a mechanism it doesn't contain is a real accuracy problem a reviewer would catch.

## claim 3: naive git-commit recency as an expertise signal has the same rival-density failure

false alarm on the direction the search was asked to check — nothing found argues "who touched it most recently" works fine in practice, contradicting candidate 3's skepticism. what turned up instead corroborates that skepticism, from two independent angles, and surfaces a separate citation problem worth fixing before the corroboration is used.

**corroborating, not threatening:** meta's own production post-mortem on its first reviewer-recommender, revrecv1, found it was "purely based on who had last modified the lines in the current diff," and it kept suggesting reviewers "who had moved onto other projects because they had edited the files in the past" — fixed only by moving to a multi-feature model, which improved correct-reviewer recall by 14 points, ran 14x faster, and got picked 27% more often by authors. [tudor gîrbă's own empirical check](http://www.tudorgirba.com/blog/yesterday-s-weather) of the "yesterday's weather" heuristic (recently-changed code predicts what changes next) found his own initial skepticism was right — it "does not necessarily apply to all systems." neither is a direct replication of candidate 3's rival-density mechanism (meta's failure is about turnover/staleness, gîrbă's is about system-to-system variance), but both are real, independently-arrived-at voices skeptical of naive recency-as-expertise-signal, for reasons adjacent to but distinct from rival-density dilution.

**the real problem:** candidate 3 attributes to codecv (ieee scam 2022) the claim that "first authorship and recency of modification are the strongest predictors of who understands a piece of code." checking this: that specific finding traces to montandon et al. (msr 2019, "identifying experts in software libraries and frameworks among github users"), a paper codecv cites in its own related work rather than codecv's original contribution — worth fixing the attribution regardless of the next point. more importantly, two independent secondary descriptions of that paper's result word it as "first authorship and recency of modification are the variables with the highest positive and negative correlations with source code knowledge, respectively" — recency of modification is the single most negatively correlated variable with measured expertise in that dataset, not a second positive predictor running alongside first authorship the way candidate 3's abstract and section 4 currently describe it. (direction and attribution confirmed against montandon et al. 2019 and the cury, avelino, santos neto & valente 2024 *information and software technology* 170, 107445 follow-up — verified 2026-09-01, and propagated as a fix into [[2026-09-01 prior exposure as an implicit edge - the link between recency reranking and code-authorship expertise]], which had the same old wrong version.)

if that direction holds against the primary source, candidate 3 is currently understating its own case in one place and overstating it in another: the literature it cites isn't saying "recency of modification predicts expertise, with the same dilution risk as this paper's own recency signal" — it's saying recency of modification predicts the absence of expertise, plausibly for a different mechanism entirely (whoever most recently touched a file may be the newest, least experienced person currently learning it or patching someone else's bug, not the person who understands it best) that has nothing to do with rival-density dilution. this doesn't undercut the rank-flip proof or the git-repo rival-density measurement, which stand on their own math and their own commit-timestamp data — but it does mean the cross-domain "literature already found the naive version has the same failure mode" framing needs the primary source checked before submission, since right now it's built on a possibly-inverted reading of its own citation.

## what this changes

claims 1 and 2 stand as written — nothing found here beats the head-to-head test candidate 3 already ran, and the rrf literature defends a different combination problem than the one candidate 3 measured. [[2026-09-01 candidate 3 paper draft outline]]'s limitations section has been updated with three additions: the re3 citation needs removing or replacing (wrong mechanism, withdrawn paper), zorro is worth adding as a third production multiplicative-wins counterexample alongside elastic and temporal-rag with the same ablation caveat, and tofallis's veto-property argument is worth citing and explicitly distinguished from candidate 3's non-veto mechanism.

claim 3's mechanism also stands, but its supporting citation needs a primary-source check before submission: the codecv/montandon attribution and, more importantly, the correlation direction for "recency of modification" should be verified against montandon et al. (msr 2019) or its 2024 information and software technology follow-up before the abstract and section 4 keep describing it as a second positive predictor alongside first authorship.
