# SERT Occupancy

**SERT occupancy** refers to the percentage of **Serotonin Transporters (SERT)** in the brain that are blocked by an SSRI (such as [[sertraline]]) at a given dose.

## What is SERT?

The **Serotonin Transporter (SERT / 5-HTT)** is a protein embedded in the presynaptic membrane of serotonergic neurons. Its job is to **reuptake [[serotonin]] (5-HT)** from the synaptic cleft back into the presynaptic neuron, effectively terminating the serotonin signal.

SSRIs work by **blocking SERT**, which prevents reuptake and increases the concentration of serotonin available in the synapse.

## How is SERT Occupancy Measured?

SERT occupancy is measured in vivo using **Positron Emission Tomography (PET)** brain imaging with radioligands (e.g. ¹¹C-DASB) that bind to SERT. By comparing scans before and after SSRI administration, researchers calculate the percentage of transporters occupied by the drug.

## Dose-Occupancy Relationship

The relationship between SSRI dose and SERT occupancy follows a **hyperbolic (Emax) curve**, not a linear one:

- **Low doses** (e.g. sertraline 12.5–25 mg) already achieve 50–66% occupancy — a disproportionately large jump from 0 mg.
- **Moderate doses** (e.g. sertraline 50 mg) reach the **~80% therapeutic threshold**, which is the occupancy level associated with optimal antidepressant response.
- **Higher doses** (e.g. 100–200 mg) add only a few extra percentage points (82–87%) because most receptors are already blocked — classic **receptor saturation / diminishing returns**.

## The ~80% Therapeutic Threshold

PET studies consistently show that **~80% SERT occupancy** is the inflection point for clinical antidepressant efficacy. Below this level, response rates drop; above it, additional occupancy adds minimal benefit for depression while side-effect burden rises linearly with dose.

This threshold is why **50 mg sertraline** is considered the standard therapeutic dose for Major Depressive Disorder — it achieves ~80% occupancy at the lowest effective dose.

## Why Some Conditions Need Higher Occupancy

Conditions like **[[Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder|OCD]]** and severe **[[Panic Disorder]]** often require doses that push occupancy to 85–87%, not because the extra SERT blockade is directly responsible, but because the higher systemic drug levels drive:
- Greater **synaptic serotonin density** over longer periods
- Deeper penetration into **subcortical structures** (e.g. basal ganglia)
- Sufficient stimulation to **desensitise downstream post-synaptic receptors** (e.g. 5-HT2C)
