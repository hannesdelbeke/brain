A framework for deep understanding: test whether you actually understand an idea by explaining it in plain language without jargon, as if teaching a child.

### The Core Principle
There is a huge difference between knowing the *name* of something and *knowing* something. Jargon often masks confusion. When you force yourself to explain a complex topic using simple, everyday words, you instantly expose where your understanding breaks down.

### The Four Steps
1. **Pick a concept**
2. **Teach it to a 12-year-old:** Explain how it works in plain language. If you must use a technical term, you have to ground it with a simple analogy.
3. **Find the gaps:** The moment you get stuck or hide behind complex words, you've hit a blind spot. Return to the source material and re-study that specific part.
4. **Simplify and refine:** Rewrite the explanation until it flows smoothly and the core mechanics feel intuitive.

### Why It Works
Reading or watching an explanation creates the *fluency illusion*—it feels easy because someone else did the hard work. Writing your own explanation from scratch forces active recall and pinpoints the exact sentences where your mental model is missing a step.
