Steve Jobs enforced extreme discipline around meetings, product scope, and accountability at Apple to avoid corporate bloat and maintain startup agility.

## 1. Directly Responsible Individual (DRI)
Every single project, agenda item, and deliverable at Apple has a named DRI.
- Exactly one person is publicly accountable for each outcome.
- Eliminates shared committee ambiguity ("if everyone owns it, nobody owns it").
- The DRI has the authority to make decisions and the responsibility to deliver.

## 2. Ruthless Meeting Pruning
Keep meetings small and strictly relevant.
- Anyone not contributing directly to the agenda is asked to leave or uninvited.
- Jobs famously removed attendees from meetings on the spot if their presence lacked clear purpose.
- Smaller rooms force higher intellectual engagement and eliminate passive spectators.

## 3. Ban on Prepared Slide Decks
Jobs disliked canned presentation slides in internal collaborative meetings.
- Preferred live product prototypes, whiteboard debates, and freeform intellectual sparring.
- "People who know what they are talking about don't need PowerPoint."

## 4. Focus Means Saying "No"
True focus is not deciding what to work on; it is rejecting hundreds of other good ideas.
- Saying no to secondary features and non-essential meetings preserves deep creative bandwidth for the primary goal.
