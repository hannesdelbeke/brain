Scientific validity comes from methodology, transparency, and reproducible [[data]] — not university affiliation. You can turn personal observations, vault findings, or experiments with participants into a citable study.

## Where to publish

You don't need academic credentials or an `.edu` address:

- [[Zenodo]] — Run by CERN and OpenAIRE. Accepts papers, datasets, and code from anyone across all fields. Assigns a permanent, citable DOI.
- **OSF Preprints** (Open Science Framework) — Ideal for behavioral, social, cognitive, and psychology experiments. Free and open to independent researchers.
- **SSRN & ResearchGate** — Broad open repositories for working papers and domain research.
- **Open access journals** — Most peer-reviewed journals accept independent submissions. List your affiliation as *Independent Researcher, [City, Country]*.
- **Public vault / whitepaper** — Publish directly on your public site or blog with linked GitHub datasets.

## Study structure

Following the standard IMRAD structure makes findings readable and credible:

- **Abstract** — 1-paragraph summary of the question, method, key metric, and main takeaway.
- **Introduction & hypothesis** — What pattern you observed, background context, and what specific hypothesis you tested.
- **Methodology** — Exact protocol so others can replicate it. Document sample size ($N$), participant selection, duration, tools or prompts used, and control vs experimental setup.
- **Results** — Raw metrics, charts, and statistical analysis (effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values).
- **Discussion & limitations** — Confounding factors, sample bias (e.g. tech peers vs general public), placebo effects, and open questions.
- **Data availability** — Link to open, anonymized CSV/JSON files and analysis code.

## Ethics and testing with people

When testing on other people, follow basic legal and ethical rules:

- **Informed consent** — Tell participants what is being tested, why, how long it takes, and ensure they can leave or delete their data anytime.
- **Data privacy & anonymization** — Strip personal identifiers (names, emails, IP addresses). Assign random participant IDs (`P01`, `P02`) before sharing.
- **Ethics review (IRB)** — Traditional biomedical and invasive psychological journals require institutional ethics approval (IRB). For non-invasive observational tests, surveys, cognitive habits, UX research, or self-experimentation ($N=1$), publishing via Zenodo, OSF, or open whitepapers with informed consent is standard.

## Building credibility

- **Pre-register the protocol** — Register your hypothesis and methodology on OSF before gathering data. This proves you didn't cherry-pick results or p-hack after the fact.
- **Open data & scripts** — Publish raw data alongside your Python/R analysis scripts so anyone can re-run your calculations.
- **State limitations clearly** — Being honest about small sample sizes and selection bias builds more credibility than over-claiming results.
