drinking alcohol has more cons than pros, don't start or limit intake.

CONS
- Alcohol causes changes in the function of the [[kidneys]] and makes them less able to filter the [[blood]]
- increase cancer risk
- makes you more depressed  ([20](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/19255375), [21](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/19486728), [22](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/18855807)).
- increase accident & violence rate
- affects your [[brain]]
- disrupts your sleep quality
- empty calories leads to weight gain

PROS
these are mostly older observational associations - newer [[Mendelian randomization]] studies and corrections for sick-quitter bias (people who already quit drinking due to declining health get counted as "abstainers", inflating the apparent benefit of moderate drinking) have undercut most of them, and the 2018 [Lancet Global Burden of Disease alcohol study](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2) concluded no level of alcohol consumption improves health
- alcohol can raise your [[high-density lipoprotein|HDL]]  ([42](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/12217928)), but HDL-raising drugs have failed to cut cardiovascular events, so this hasn't held up as a real health benefit
- Cut the risk of [[diabetes]]  ([44](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15735217)), though later Mendelian randomization work found no protective effect of alcohol on diabetes risk
- it can make you more social
- moderate drinking might reduce dementia ([16](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/12636463), [17](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/20617045), [18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/9296132)), but this is exactly the kind of finding sick-quitter bias produces - people who already have early cognitive decline tend to stop drinking first, making abstainers look worse off

bad for your [[health]]
