there's a difference between low levels, and a deficiency.
e.g. You don't need to take creatine [[supplement|supplements]] as a [[vegetarian]], but will do better if you do.

vegetarians have lower levels of
- [[creatine]]
	- veggies have less in their muscles than omnivores, it also enhances concentration in veggies.
- [[taurine]]
	- veggies have less in their muscles than omnivores
	- Taurine is only found in animal products such as seafood, poultry, and dairy products [65](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/2352336)
- [[cholesterol]]
	- cholesterol is needed to make sex hormones such as [[pregnenolone]], DHEA, and [[testosterone]].
- [[carnosine]]
	- Carnosine is only found in meat and dairy products but can be formed in the body from the amino acids histidine and beta-alanine. Vegetarians have less carnosine in their muscles than meat eaters
- [[carnitine]] 
	- Since carnitine is primarily found in meats and dairy products, vegans are at risk for carnitine deficiency [50](http://ajcn.nutrition.org/content/50/2/301.abstract)
	- in a small 12-week trial of 24 men (16 vegetarians, 8 omnivores), carnitine supplementation increased skeletal [[muscle]] carnitine stores by approximately 13% in the [[vegetarian|vegetarians]], but not in the omnivores, though it made no measurable difference to performance in either group ([study](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Carnitine-HealthProfessional/))

possible low levels
- Protein, [[essential amino acids]]
- [[essential fatty acid]] Pre-formed Omega-3 Fatty Acids [[docosahexaenoic acid|DHA]] and [[eicosapentaenoic acid|EPA]] 
	- These are in nuts and seeds, and mainly in [[fish]].
	- ALA found in flax/chia/walnuts does not convert well into DHA [74](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/m/pubmed/19269799/)]
- [[Zinc]]
	- Many plant foods that contain zinc also contain [[phytate]], which inhibits [zinc](https://selfhacked.com/blog/literally-everything-wanted-know-zinc-backed-science/) absorption [24](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/25439135). a review modelling zinc bioavailability across diet compositions estimated **vegetarian diets reduce** zinc absorption by about 35% compared to an omnivorous diet — a calculated estimate from the review, not a number measured in one trial [23](http://ajcn.nutrition.org/content/78/3/633S.long)
- [[iron]]
- [[Vitamin D]]
	- [[vitamin D3]]
	- **Vegans’ dietary intake of** [**vitamin D**](https://selfhacked.com/blog/natural-ways-to-increase-calcitrol-and-vitamin-d-receptor-gene-expression/) **is below the recommended range**. Inadequate intake can decrease bone mineral density and cause bone abnormalities. in a prospective longitudinal study of 181 postmenopausal Asian women (105 vegans, 105 omnivores at baseline), 73% of vegans compared to 46% of omnivores had vitamin D deficiency, but veganism was not associated with bone loss or fracture [13](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/21811293) — [14](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/21092700) is an unrelated single-family case report and [15](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/21872800) a general review on vitamin D and bone, not the same study
- [[Calcium]]
	- In vegans, the dietary intake of calcium and vitamin D can be below the recommended range. This may cause a decrease in bone mineral density and increase the risk of bone abnormalities, though a review of the literature calls the calcium/vitamin D-to-fracture link inconclusive, and the two underlying studies are small — a single-family case report and an 8-person short-term balance study [14](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/21092700), [27](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/12491091), [28](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/16942519)
- [[iodine]]
	- Iodine is more commonly found in animal food products than those of plant origin. one small study of 81 Slovakian adults (15 vegans, 31 vegetarians, 35 omnivores) found 80% of the vegans had iodine deficiency (urinary iodine under 100 µg/l), compared to 9% of those who ate both meat and plants [39](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/12748410)
- [[vitamin A]]
	- Plants contain [beta-carotene](https://selfhacked.com/blog/beta-carotene/), which can be converted to active Vitamin A, but there are multiple factors that can affect the conversion rate and beta-carotene absorption. Therefore, it is possible for vegans to be vitamin A deficient [19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2855261/), [20](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5372909/), [21](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4628270/).
- vitamin B
	- Riboflavin / [[vitamin B2]]
		- in a study of 132 Thai vegetarians, 25% had a riboflavin deficiency — but so did 22% of the study's non-vegetarian controls, so this particular study didn't find it vegetarian-specific [69](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/1797957), [71](https://www.ernaehrungs-umschau.de/fileadmin/Ernaehrungs-Umschau/pdfs/pdf_2016/04_16/EU04_2016_Special_DGE_eng_final.pdf)
	- vitamin B3
		- in a Swiss study of 53 vegetarians, 34% were deficient in niacin [68](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/26502280)
	- [[choline]] / vitamin b4
		- Vegans may be at risk for choline deficiency since most food sources are either meat, dairy, or seafood products [26](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/80400525/Data/Choline/Choln02.pdf).
	- [[vitamin B6]]
		- 30-60% of veggies are deficient — 29% in a 132-person Thai study [69](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/1797957) and 58% in a 53-person Swiss study [68](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/26502280), two small studies rather than one population-wide rate
	- [[vitamin B12]]
		- Because natural sources are only found in meat, **vegans are frequently vitamin B12 deficient** [5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/12816782)
- [[selenium]]
	- Vegans in the U.S. appear to have adequate intakes of selenium. Low intakes and lower blood concentrations have been reported in vegans and vegetarians in other areas of the world where soil selenium is low.
- [[Glycine]]
	- Vegans don’t ingest as much glycine from the diet as meat eaters (even though their blood levels are higher) [56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4705437/)
