# Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

**Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)** is a chronic mental health condition characterised by persistent, unwanted **obsessions** (intrusive thoughts, images, or urges) and **compulsions** (repetitive behaviours or mental acts performed to reduce the anxiety caused by obsessions).

## Core Features

- **Obsessions:** Recurrent, intrusive thoughts that cause marked anxiety — e.g. contamination fears, harm-related doubts, need for symmetry/order.
- **Compulsions:** Repetitive behaviours (hand washing, checking, counting, arranging) or mental rituals (praying, mentally reviewing) performed to neutralise the obsession.
- The cycle is **self-reinforcing**: compulsions temporarily reduce anxiety, which negatively reinforces the obsessive loop.

## Neurobiology

OCD is strongly associated with dysfunction in the **Cortico-Striatal-Thalamo-Cortical (CSTC) loop** — a circuit linking the orbitofrontal cortex, caudate nucleus, globus pallidus, and thalamus. In OCD, this loop is **hyperactive**, creating persistent "error signals" that the brain cannot resolve.

Key neurotransmitter involvement:
- **Serotonin (5-HT):** SSRIs are first-line treatment. Higher doses are typically required compared to depression — see [[sertraline dosing]]
- **Dopamine:** Augmentation with low-dose antipsychotics is sometimes used for SSRI-resistant OCD.
- **Glutamate:** Emerging research implicates glutamatergic dysfunction in the CSTC circuit.

## Treatment

### Pharmacological
- **SSRIs** are first-line, but OCD typically requires **higher doses** and **longer trial periods** (8–12 weeks) than depression.
- Sertraline therapeutic range for OCD: **150–200 mg/day** (vs. 50 mg for depression) — see [[SERT occupancy]].
- [[Clopidogrel|Clomipramine]] (a tricyclic) is sometimes used for resistant cases.

### Psychological
- **CBT with Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP)** is the gold-standard psychological treatment.
- ERP involves gradual, structured exposure to obsession triggers while preventing the compulsive response, allowing the anxiety to habituate naturally.

## Prevalence

- Affects approximately **1–2%** of the global population.
- Onset typically in **late adolescence to early adulthood**, though childhood onset is common.
- Equally common in men and women, but men tend to have earlier onset.
