**Emotional disengagement** is a relationship dynamic characterized by the gradual erosion of positive affect, humor, affection, empathy, and sexual intimacy, while overt conflict remains relatively low.

Empirically identified by John Gottman and Robert Levenson in their 14-year longitudinal study (*Journal of Marriage and Family*, 2000, [DOI: 10.1111/j.1741-3737.2000.00737.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1741-3737.2000.00737.x)).

---

## Early vs. Late Divorce Trajectories

Gottman and Levenson's research demonstrated that couples separate along two distinct time curves:

| Trajectory | Primary Driver | Average Dissolution Timeline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Early Dissolution** | High negative affect: criticism, defensiveness, contempt, stonewalling during conflict. | **5.6 years** post-commitment |
| **Late Dissolution** | **Emotional Disengagement:** Absence of positive affect, mutual humor, physical spark, and shared curiosity. | **16.2 years** post-commitment |

---

## The "Roommate" Trap

In emotionally disengaged partnerships:
* Daily logistics, bills, and household routines function smoothly.
* Both partners retreat into solo hobbies, career focus, or screens to avoid confronting the emotional void.
* Because overt fights are rare, the dynamic creates powerful [[public/relationship inertia|relationship inertia]], keeping couples in holding patterns for decades until midlife or late-stage separation (Susan Brown & I-Fen Lin, *The Gray Divorce Revolution*, 2012, [DOI: 10.1093/geronb/gbs089](https://doi.org/10.1093/geronb/gbs089)).
