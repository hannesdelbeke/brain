# Panic Disorder

**Panic Disorder** is an [[anxiety disorder]] characterised by recurrent, unexpected **panic attacks** — sudden surges of intense fear or discomfort that peak within minutes — accompanied by persistent worry about future attacks or maladaptive behavioural changes to avoid them.

## Core Features

- **Panic attacks:** Acute episodes featuring a combination of physical and cognitive symptoms:
    - Pounding heart / palpitations
    - Sweating, trembling, shortness of breath
    - Chest pain, nausea, dizziness
    - Derealization or depersonalisation
    - Fear of losing control or dying
- **Anticipatory anxiety:** Ongoing worry about when the next attack will happen.
- **Avoidance behaviour:** Avoiding places or situations associated with past attacks, which can progress to **agoraphobia**.

## Neurobiology

Panic Disorder involves dysregulation of the brain's **fear network**, centred on the **amygdala**, with contributions from the **locus coeruleus** (norepinephrine), **periaqueductal grey**, and **hypothalamus**.

Key neurotransmitter involvement:
- **[[Serotonin]] (5-HT):** SSRIs are first-line pharmacotherapy. They modulate amygdala reactivity and fear-circuit sensitivity over time.
- **Norepinephrine:** Overactivation of the locus coeruleus contributes to the "fight or flight" surge during attacks.
- **GABA:** Benzodiazepines (short-term) enhance GABAergic inhibition to dampen acute panic.

## Treatment

### Pharmacological
- **SSRIs** are first-line for long-term management. Treatment typically starts at a **low dose** to avoid initial activation/worsening of anxiety.
- Sertraline starting dose: **25 mg/day** (vs. 50 mg for depression), titrating up — see [[sertraline dosing]].
- Severe or treatment-resistant cases may require doses up to **150–200 mg/day** — see [[SERT occupancy]].
- **Benzodiazepines** (e.g. clonazepam) may be used short-term for acute symptom relief, but carry dependence risk.

### Psychological
- **CBT** is the gold-standard psychological treatment, particularly:
    - **Interoceptive exposure:** Deliberately inducing panic-like sensations (e.g. hyperventilation, spinning) in a safe context to reduce fear of the sensations themselves.
    - **Cognitive restructuring:** Challenging catastrophic misinterpretations of bodily sensations.

## Prevalence

- Affects approximately **2–3%** of the global population.
- Onset typically in **late adolescence to mid-30s**.
- **Twice as common in women** as in men.
- High comorbidity with **agoraphobia**, [[depression]], and other anxiety disorders.
