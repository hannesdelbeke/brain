Core patterns and structural lessons from high-signal public [[digital garden|digital gardens]] and [[Zettelkasten]] repositories:

### Title by Principle Over Category
- Name notes as propositions and mental models (e.g. *Chesterton's fence*, *Hedonic treadmill*, *Purpose of a system is what it does*), not generic folder buckets like `Philosophy` or `Books`.
- Lets you drop links naturally inline as active thoughts rather than siloed references.

### Capture Subjective Reflection, Not Just Facts
- Include emotional context, doubts, and why an idea resonated alongside objective claims.
- Facts are easily retrieved by AI; personal intuition and reasoning context cannot be reconstructed after the fact.

### Progressive Maturity Stages
- Use lifecycle stages (Seedling $\rightarrow$ Budding $\rightarrow$ Evergreen) to lower capture friction.
- Raw drafts enter the vault without guilt or polluting mature concept notes.

### Bridge High-level Philosophy with Low-level Tech
- Connect technical architecture (systems design, code) directly to epistemology and ethics.
- The highest-value insights occur at the intersection of technical workflows and human thinking.

### Tool Longevity and Minimalism
- Prioritize plain [[Markdown]] and standard-library scripts over fragile, plugin-heavy ecosystems.
- Vaults built on simple, open formats survive tool churn and remain portable across decades.

### Curation Over Hoarding
- Small, opinionated, deeply linked vaults consistently outperform bloated vaults filled with unread highlights.
- Dense, curated graph topology provides better context for human thinking and agent retrieval.
