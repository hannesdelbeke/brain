Paul Graham's 2009 essay distinguishing the two conflicting modes of organizing time in organizations: the **Manager's Schedule** and the **Maker's Schedule**.

## 1. The Manager's Schedule
Organized in 1-hour time blocks across the calendar.
- Typical for executives, product managers, and administrators whose primary job is coordination and communication.
- A 30-minute or 1-hour meeting simply occupies one slot; switching between slots has low friction.

## 2. The Maker's Schedule
Organized in units of half-days or full days (3–4+ hours of uninterrupted focus).
- Required by programmers, writers, technical artists, and engineers building complex architectures.
- Deep cognitive work requires holding a large mental model in working memory.

## 3. The Collision & Friction
When a manager schedules a 30-minute meeting in the middle of a maker's afternoon:
- To the manager, it is a minor 30-minute block.
- To the maker, it fractures the entire 4-hour sprint into two unusable fragments, destroying focus before and after the interruption.

## Heuristics for Protection
- **Office Hours & Clustered Blocks:** Cluster meetings at the beginning or end of the day to leave uninterrupted mornings/afternoons.
- **Asynchronous First:** Resolve coordination via short PRs, written tickets, or chat rather than synchronous meetings.
- **No-Meeting Days:** Designate recurring full days reserved exclusively for maker deep work.
